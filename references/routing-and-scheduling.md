# Routing and scheduling

## City trips

Use one main area and at most two adjacent areas per day. Compare walking, bicycle, metro, bus, taxi, and driving for each leg. Use the mode that best fits time, transfers, weather, people, luggage, and price. Keep short walks; use a taxi when it removes a disproportionate transfer.

Store opening, closing, last entry, duration, best time, travel, and queue buffer for every scheduled stop. A place is schedulable only when its entire useful visit fits the time window.

## Province-scale and road trips

Build the spine before the details:

1. Select arrival and departure cities from available transport and route direction.
2. Order regional clusters as an open-jaw or one-way corridor.
3. Choose overnight bases and expose transfer-only days.
4. Add scenic loops and day trips only when they do not create unsafe or wasteful repetition.

“No backtracking” means minimizing long repeated corridors. A short return to the same base can be better when it avoids one-night moves, night driving, unreliable roads, or inferior lodging.

## Rest and safety

For driving legs, schedule a real stop every 90–120 minutes where feasible. A rest point should have a toilet and safe parking; prefer fuel or charging and food. Add a longer lunch stop on four-hour-plus transfer days. Show rest points in the itinerary and route map.

Protect the first half-day after a long flight or train. Avoid mountain, desert, or unfamiliar rural roads after dark. Add buffers for refueling, checkpoints, livestock, roadworks, weather, and holiday traffic. Mark high-altitude acclimatization, border-document, seasonal-road, and limited-signal risks when relevant.

## Route switches

Create an explicit switch when route viability depends on dates or conditions:

- seasonal road open → main scenic corridor;
- seasonal road closed → all-season highway or rail/flight alternative;
- severe weather → indoor, lower-altitude, or shortened day;
- reservation unavailable → verified nearby substitute;
- holiday congestion → earlier departure, different entrance, or overnight-base change.

State the decision date and what evidence triggers the switch.

## Daily rhythm

Match the requested pace. A relaxed day needs recovery gaps, normal meals, and an early finish after a long transfer. Avoid stacking multiple similar attractions just because they are nearby. Two culturally distinct temples, museums, or landscapes can coexist when their roles and visit lengths are meaningfully different; otherwise select one and use the other as a backup.
